## 🧠 Why Weather Is Central to Energy Trading

- Energy traders often treat **weather forecasts with near-obsessive importance**
    
- Weather is a **primary short- and medium-term driver** of:
    
    - Electricity prices
        
    - Natural gas prices
        
    - Heating oil prices
        
- Most energy trading desks start the day with a **weather briefing**
    
- Unlike most modern sectors, **commodity markets remain highly weather-dependent**
    

> Weather → Physical reality → Supply/Demand → Price

---

## 🔗 Channels of Transmission: Weather → Market Prices

**Channels of transmission** = all market processes through which weather conditions affect prices via:

- Producers
    
- Consumers
    
- Traders
    
- Physical infrastructure
    

### Two Core Channels

1. **Supply-side impacts**
    
2. **Demand-side impacts**
    

---

## ⚙️ Supply-Side Weather Impacts (Energy Markets)

Unlike agriculture (slow supply response), energy supply impacts are often:

- **Sudden**
    
- **Localized**
    
- **Infrastructure-driven**
    

### Examples of Supply Disruptions

- 🌫️ **Fog**
    
    - Example: Houston Ship Channel
        
    - Disrupts shipping of crude/products → temporary shortages
        
- 🌊 **High sea levels**
    
    - Kelp clogging nuclear plant water intakes (California)
        
    - Reduces electricity generation
        
- 🌋 **Volcanic eruptions**
    
    - Inject ash/dust into stratosphere
        
    - Alters global weather patterns → indirect energy impacts
        
- 🌊 **River conditions**
    
    - Low water → cooling water unavailable for coal/nuclear plants
        
    - Floods → barge traffic disrupted (coal, crude, gasoline)
        
- 🛢️ **Water pollution**
    
    - 2010 Gulf oil spill → threatened refineries & coastal plants
        
- 💧 **Drought**
    
    - Limits water availability for hydraulic fracturing
        
    - Reduces hydroelectric output
        

> ⚠️ Even “small” local events can create **highly profitable trading opportunities**

---

## 🔁 Cross-Market & Global Transmission

Weather impacts **do not stay local**:

### Example: Europe 2005

- Drought in France & Spain:
    
    - Reduced hydro + nuclear output
        
    - Increased LNG demand
        
    - LNG diverted from US → US gas prices affected
        

➡️ **Global energy market integration amplifies weather effects**

---

## 🔥 Demand-Side Weather Impacts

### Electricity Demand

- **US:** Summer peaks (air conditioning)
    
- **Europe:** Historically winter-driven (heating)
    
- Trend: Increasing summer sensitivity due to AC adoption
    

### Natural Gas Demand

- **Winter:** Heating load (US & Europe)
    
- **Summer (US):**
    
    - Gas-fired peaker plants run during heat waves
        
    - Gas demand spikes due to electricity load
        

### Heating Oil

- Highly sensitive to **winter temperatures**
    
- Major markets: US, Europe, Asia
    

---

## 👥 Role of Weather Teams in Trading Firms

Weather forecasting alone is **not enough**.

### Core Responsibilities

- Short- & medium-term forecast interpretation
    
- Seasonal outlook development
    
- Historical weather databases for:
    
    - Demand modeling
        
    - Weather derivatives pricing
        

### Key Insight

> The _same_ weather forecast can have **very different price impacts**, depending on:

- Infrastructure conditions
    
- Inventory levels
    
- Market tightness

## 🧮 Weather Forecasting Models

### Why Models Are Central

- Weather models require:
    
    - Massive computing power
        
    - Huge datasets
        
- Mostly run by:
    
    - Governments
        
    - Quasi-government agencies
        

### Major Institutions

- NOAA / NCEP (US)
    
- Environment Canada
    
- ECMWF (Europe)
    
- UK Met Office
    

Energy firms typically:

- Consume model outputs via consultants
    
- Compete on **speed**, **interpretation**, and **judgment**
    

---

## 🌍 Major Numerical Weather Models

### 🇺🇸 Global Forecast System (GFS)

- Run by NOAA / NCEP
    
- Frequency: 4x daily
    
- Horizon: Up to 16 days
    
- Useful trading horizon: **5–7 days**
    
- Free & publicly available
    
- Known as: **“American model”**
    

---

### 🇨🇦 GEM (Global Environmental Multiscale Model)

- Canadian model
    
- Very popular among energy traders
    
- Known as: **“Canadian model”**
    

---

### 🇪🇺 European Models

- **UKMET** (UK Met Office)
    
- **ECMWF**
    
    - 10-day deterministic forecast
        
    - Highly respected
        
    - Known as: **“European model”**
        

> Traders often say: _“The European is bullish”_ or _“The GFS flipped colder”_

---

## 🎲 Ensemble Forecasting & Chaos

### Core Problem

- Weather is a **non-linear, chaotic system**
    
- Small errors → large forecast divergence  
    (Lorenz, 1963)
    

### Ensemble Method

- Run model many times with slightly varied inputs
    
- Outcomes:
    
    - Tight convergence → high confidence
        
    - “Spaghetti plots” → low confidence
        

This is effectively **Monte Carlo simulation for weather**

## 🌐 Weather Anomalies (Seasonal & Structural Patterns)

Anomalies = deviations from long-term climatology  
Used for **seasonal outlooks**, not daily trading

⚠️ Multiple anomalies can overlap → high uncertainty

---

## 🌊 Key Weather Anomalies

### 🌀 NAO – North Atlantic Oscillation

- Pressure difference between Iceland & Azores
    
- Strongly affects:
    
    - European winters
        
    - Scandinavian hydro production
        

**Positive NAO**

- Warm, wet Northern Europe
    
- More hydro generation (Norway, Sweden)
    

**Negative NAO**

- Cold Europe
    
- Reduced hydro inflows
    

---

### 🌧️ MJO – Madden–Julian Oscillation

- Tropical disturbance
    
- Cycle: ~30–60 days
    
- Influences:
    
    - ENSO development
        
    - US winter temperatures
        
    - West Coast rainfall
        

**Key phenomenon:** _Pineapple Express_

- Heavy rain events in US West Coast
    
- Critical for hydro & power traders
    

---

### 🌊 PDO – Pacific Decadal Oscillation

- Long-term (20–30 years)
    
- Warm vs cold ocean phases
    

**Warm PDO**

- Less Pacific Northwest precipitation
    
- Lower hydro output
    

**Cold PDO**

- More precipitation
    
- Higher hydro output
    

---

### 🌍 ENSO – El Niño / La Niña (Most Important)

#### El Niño

- Weak trade winds
    
- Warmer eastern Pacific
    
- Fewer Atlantic hurricanes
    
- US impacts:
    
    - Wetter Gulf states
        
    - Warmer northern winters
        

#### La Niña

- Strong trade winds
    
- Cooler eastern Pacific
    
- More Atlantic hurricanes
    
- Colder northern US winters
    

### Key Indexes

- **SOI (Southern Oscillation Index)**
    
- **SST anomalies (El Niño 3.4 region)**
    

These charts should be **monthly staples on trading desks**

## 🌀 Hurricanes: Prediction & Trading Impact

### Hurricane Season

- June 1 – Nov 30
    
- Seasonal forecasts released months in advance
    

### Major Forecast Providers

- Colorado State University
    
- UCL (Mark Saunders group)
    

### Forecast Metrics

- Named storms
    
- Hurricane days
    
- Major hurricane days
    
- Probability of Gulf Coast landfall
    

⚠️ **Forecasts are probabilistic — often overinterpreted by traders**

---

## 🏗️ Hurricane Impact Channels

### Supply Effects

- Offshore platform shutdowns
    
- Pipeline damage
    
- Refinery shutdowns
    
- Long restart times
    

### Demand Effects

- Population displacement
    
- Industrial shutdowns
    
- Grid damage → lower power demand
    

➡️ Prices may rise **or fall**, depending on dominance

---

## 📏 Hurricane Strength Measures

### Saffir–Simpson Scale

- Categories 1–5 (wind speed based)
    
- Criticism: Discrete jumps exaggerate perception
    

### Carvill Hurricane Index (CHI)

- Used in CME hurricane derivatives
    
- Continuous measure:
    
    - Wind speed
        
    - Storm radius
        

---

## 🧩 Post-Landfall Chaos

- Fragmented markets
    
- Conflicting information
    
- Stranded gas
    
- Regional price spikes & collapses
    
- Trader decision-making under extreme uncertainty
    

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- Weather is a **first-order driver** of short-term energy prices
    
- Understanding **transmission channels** is more important than raw forecasts
    
- Weather forecasting is:
    
    - Science + experience + judgment
        
- Weather anomalies shape **seasonal positioning**
    
- Hurricanes create **non-linear, asymmetric risks**
    
- Weather data feeds:
    
    - Demand models
        
    - Price forecasts
        
    - Weather derivatives
