## 📘 Normal Backwardation (Risk Premium Theory)

### Definition

Normal backwardation is the theory that **forward prices are systematically below expected future spot prices** because hedgers pay a **risk premium** to speculators to absorb price risk.

---

### Core Equation

F(t,T)=Et[ST]−PF(t,T) = \mathbb{E}_t[S_T] - PF(t,T)=Et​[ST​]−P

- `F(t,T)` → Forward price
    
- `E[S_T]` → Expected spot at maturity
    
- `P` → Risk premium (positive under normal backwardation)
    

---

### Natural Market Participants

#### Natural Shorts

- Long physical exposure
    
- Hedge by **selling forwards**
    
- Examples:
    
    - Oil & gas producers
        
    - Farmers
        

Characteristics:

- Risk-averse
    
- Revenue-sensitive
    
- Often capital constrained
    

#### Natural Longs

- Need commodity for operations
    
- Hedge by **buying forwards**
    
- Examples:
    
    - Utilities
        
    - Refineries
        
    - Industrial users
        

---

### Structural Imbalance

- Producer hedging demand > consumer hedging demand
    
- Excess forward selling pressure
    
- Speculators must absorb imbalance
    

---

### Role of Speculators

- Take price risk voluntarily
    
- Require compensation
    
- Compensation = **risk premium**
    
- Expect profits via convergence at maturity
    

---

### Normal Backwardation vs Curve Shape

|Concept|Definition|
|---|---|
|Curve backwardation|Spot > forward|
|Normal backwardation|Forward < expected spot|

⚠️ These are **not the same thing**.

---

### Contango + Normal Backwardation (Possible)

Conditions:

- High inventories
    
- Depressed spot prices
    
- Higher expected future spot
    

Result:

- Spot < Forward → Contango
    
- Forward < Expected Spot → Normal backwardation
    

---

### Role of Inventories

- Inventories decouple spot from expectations
    
- High inventories → low spot
    
- Low inventories → high spot
    
- Risk premium can exist in both regimes
    

(→ see [[Storage Theory]])

---

### Keynes’s Key Insights

- Backwardation does NOT require supply shortages
    
- Even balanced markets should show:
    
    - Spot > forward by hedging cost
        
- If hedging cost is too high:
    
    - Producers may stop hedging
        

---

### Empirical Implications

1. Forward prices converge to spot at maturity
    
2. Speculator net longs mirror producer net shorts
    
3. Speculators earn positive average returns
    

---

### Practical Limitations

- Markets are heterogeneous
    
- Seasonality distorts results
    
- Leverage ignored
    
- Producers often speculate
    
- Financialisation weakens original assumptions
    

---

### Trader’s Interpretation

- Normal backwardation = **who pays for risk**
    
- Not guaranteed
    
- Regime-dependent
    
- Weakens in financialised energy markets
