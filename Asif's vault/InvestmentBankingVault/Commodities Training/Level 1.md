
# **Level 1 – Commodities, Instruments & Risk Foundations**

### _What is traded, who trades it, and why risk exists_

## **Level Objective**

Understand **what commodities and instruments are traded**, **who participates across the value chain**, **what risks are embedded by design**, and **how those risks are priced, managed, and represented in systems**.

Level 1 builds the **commercial and structural foundation** on which pricing, risk, PnL, and operations depend.

---

## **Module 1 – Commodities & Instruments in Context**

### _From physical reality to tradable contracts_

**Objective**  
Explain the core commodity classes and instrument types, and why these instruments exist given physical, economic, and market constraints.

**Scope**

- Commodity classes: Agriculture, Natural Gas, Crude, Metals
    
- Physical characteristics: storability, transportability, seasonality, perishability
    
- Instrument types:
    
    - Physical deals and forward contracts
        
    - Futures and swaps
        
    - Options, options on futures, swaptions
        
    - Inventory, storage, and transport deals
        
- Physical vs financial vs hybrid exposure
    

**Key Insight**  
Instruments exist because physical commodities cannot be produced, moved, or consumed instantly.

---

## **Module 2 – Participants, Value Chain & Commercial Intent**

### _Who uses which instruments and why_

**Objective**  
Explain how different market participants use instruments to manage constraints and objectives across the value chain.

**Scope**

- Participants:
    
    - Producers
        
    - Consumers (utilities, industrials)
        
    - Merchants and trading houses
        
    - Financial institutions
        
    - Infrastructure players
        
- Upstream, midstream, downstream activities
    
- Trading entities vs operating entities
    
- Merchant vs agency business models
    

**Key Insight**  
The same instrument serves very different purposes depending on participant intent.

---

## **Module 3 – Risk Embedded, Pricing & System Representation**

### _Why risk and pricing are inseparable_

**Objective**  
Introduce how risk is embedded in instrument design, how pricing reflects market expectations, and how systems represent these concepts.

**Scope**

- Embedded risks:
    
    - Price, basis, volume, timing, operational risk
        
- Pricing approaches:
    
    - Spot, forward, index-linked, formula-based
        
- Introduction to market data dependencies
    
- High-level risk management intuition
    
- Conceptual system representation:
    
    - Instruments, entities, portfolios, internal flows in **Openlink Endur**
        

**Key Insight**  
Risk is designed into contracts, and pricing is the market’s way of expressing that risk.
